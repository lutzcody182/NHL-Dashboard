What the numbers mean

Corsi (CF / CA / CF%)
Every shot attempt: on goal, missed, or blocked. CF% is a team's share of all attempts while it's on the ice. It's the classic "who had the puck" stat. Above 50% means you're controlling play.
Fenwick (FF / FA / FF%)
Same as Corsi but without blocked shots ("unblocked attempts"). Our expected goals model is built on these, because a blocked shot's origin isn't recorded.
Expected Goals (xG, xGF, xGA, xGF%)
How many goals a shot "should" produce, based on how often similar shots go in. A 10-foot rebound might be worth 0.30 xG, and a 60-foot wrister about 0.01. Add them up and you get xGF (for) and xGA (against). xGF% is the share of expected goals, and it's the best single number here for how well a team is really playing.
High-danger chances (HDCF%)
Unblocked attempts from the inner slot (within about 22 ft and a tight angle) or close-range rebounds.
Scoring chances (SCF%)
A wider "good look" zone (about 38 ft and within 60°) plus all rebounds and rush shots. High-danger chances are a subset of these.
SH% · SV% · PDO
Shooting % plus save % (×100) is PDO. League average is about 100. Teams far above it are usually getting lucky and tend to come back down.
GSAx (Goals Saved Above Expected)
The expected goals a goalie faced minus the goals they allowed (empty-net goals excluded). A positive number means they stopped more than an average goalie would have.
G − xG / G − ixG (Finishing)
Actual goals minus expected goals. Positive means finishing better than the shot quality suggests (skill or a hot streak).
ixG · iCF
Individual expected goals and individual shot attempts: a player's own shots only.
/60
A rate per 60 minutes of ice time, so players and teams with different minutes can be compared fairly.
Even strength (EV)
Both teams have the same number of skaters, mostly 5-on-5. Analysts prefer it because power plays skew everything.
Deserve-to-win meter
We replay each game's shots 10,000 times, with every shot going in at its xG probability, and count how often each team wins. Ties are split 50/50.

How it's built

Data
All data comes live from ESPN's public API: scoreboard, standings, and full play-by-play with rink coordinates for every shot. The NHL's own API and sites like MoneyPuck and Natural Stat Trick don't let web pages fetch their data directly, so every advanced stat here is computed in your browser from the play-by-play.
Expected goals model
A logistic model using shot distance, shot angle, shot type (tip, deflection, backhand, slap, snap, wrist, wrap-around), rebounds (a shot within 3 seconds of a teammate's attempt), rush chances (a shot within 4 seconds of an event outside the offensive zone), and power play. Totals are then calibrated so league-wide xG matches league-wide goals across the games you've loaded.
Ice time
Player TOI comes from ESPN box scores. Team even-strength time is estimated as the sum of skaters' even-strength TOI ÷ 5.
Caching
Processed games are saved in your browser, so after the first load, switching ranges or coming back later is much faster. Use "Clear cache" if something looks off.
Limitations
This is an approximation. Public models like MoneyPuck and Evolving-Hockey use more inputs (pre-shot movement, score effects, shooter talent) and official NHL shift data. Expect our numbers to be directionally the same as theirs, not identical. ESPN occasionally mislabels a shot's coordinates or type, and early-season samples are noisy.
